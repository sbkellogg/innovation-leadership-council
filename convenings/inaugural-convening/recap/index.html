Tuesday at a Glance

A strong foundation—and important questions

Members aligned around several broad principles: design for varied North Carolina contexts, use evidence without rewarding application polish, preserve context, support thoughtful adaptation, broaden who gets surfaced, and connect people to more than a static repository.

Strong Convergence

Where the Council found common ground

These themes appeared across most or nearly all table groups, with little substantive alternative direction.

1

Design for equitable access

The Exchange should work across rural, urban, suburban, charter, lab, small, and larger systems—not only well-resourced or well-connected PSUs.

2

Preserve context and conditions

Share why a practice works, the resources and conditions it requires, what is essential, and what can be adapted.

3

Use clear and credible evidence

Apply transparent criteria consistently and look beyond polished applications toward meaningful evidence and impact.

4

Surface practices intentionally

Broad outreach is necessary because passive solicitation can reproduce existing visibility and access advantages.

5

Build an active exchange

The value is in connecting people to practices, practitioners, networks, supports, funders, and partners—not merely creating another clearinghouse.

6

Separate recognition from scale

A promising practice may deserve visibility before it is ready for broader adoption. Scale should depend on readiness, evidence, fit, resources, and buy-in.

Cross-Cutting Tensions

Tradeoffs the design must hold

These are not problems to eliminate once. They are tensions to revisit throughout the Exchange’s design and operation.

Focus without exclusion

Create enough focus to make solicitation understandable and comparison meaningful without screening out valuable local work.

How focused should the first cycle be while still leaving room for worthwhile practices?

Credibility without excessive burden

Gather enough evidence for a trustworthy process without favoring large systems, experienced grant writers, or teams with greater evaluation capacity.

What is the minimum information needed at each stage for a credible decision?

Adaptation without losing what works

Expect local adaptation while making the practice’s essential components and supporting conditions visible.

What must remain intact, and what can change to fit another context?

Recognition without premature scale

Celebrate promising work without implying that it is automatically ready for widespread replication or state-supported adoption.

What additional readiness, demand, evidence, and support should broader adoption require?

Draft Cross-Strand Design Principles

Eight decision rules for the work ahead

The synthesis proposed these as shared lenses across the design strands—not as eight additional criteria to score separately.

Start with a clear problem and focus

Organize the Exchange around meaningful problems or priorities that create coherence without excluding worthwhile ideas.

Ask: What problem are we solving, for whom, and is the focus clear without becoming unnecessarily restrictive?

Prioritize meaningful impact

Elevate work for its potential to improve outcomes for students, educators, families, or schools—not because it is novel for its own sake.

Ask: What should be different because this practice exists, and what evidence would show that difference?

Design for equity, access, and representation

Ensure educators and PSUs across regions, school types, sizes, communities, and resource levels can participate and benefit.

Ask: Who could be advantaged, overlooked, or unable to participate, adopt, or benefit?

Make participation worthwhile and proportionate

Ask only for what is necessary at each stage and make the value to educators and PSUs clear.

Ask: Is the burden proportionate to the decision, and what does the contributor receive in return?

Use evidence clearly, consistently, and in context

Use transparent criteria and multiple forms of evidence while accounting for maturity, context, and local capacity.

Ask: What do we know, how confident are we, and are we applying criteria consistently and fairly?

Preserve the practice, the people, and the context

Document what happened, why it worked, who did the work, what supported it, and what resources it required.

Ask: Would another educator understand both the practice and the conditions that made it successful?

Adapt with fidelity

Support local adaptation while identifying the essential elements that should be protected.

Ask: What is core and should remain intact, and what can change to fit another context?

Scale responsibly and sustainably

Move toward broader adoption only when evidence, readiness, resources, demand, implementation support, and stakeholder buy-in justify it.

Ask: Is this ready to move further, what support is required, and what could happen if we move too quickly?

Useful shorthand
“Tight on the core, loose on the rest.”

Preserve the elements that make a practice effective while allowing appropriate adaptation to local context.

Reservations and Unresolved Questions

What remains open

The synthesis intentionally preserves issues that did not reach the same level of convergence. These items require continued design work rather than being smoothed over.

General convergence

Direction with conditions

  • Create focus and coherence without becoming too narrow.
  • Keep participation practical, proportionate, and worthwhile.
  • Build a durable effort that connects to existing work.
  • Keep student, educator, family, and practitioner voices visible.
Not enough input yet

Questions needing resolution

  • Should evidence expectations vary by maturity or stage?
  • Should the Exchange use staged recognition or one common standard?
  • What shared working definition of “innovation” should guide eligibility?
Minority perspective

Practices, not packages

  • Prioritize locally grounded practices rather than commercially packaged solutions.
  • Preserve this question because it could materially affect eligibility, even though Council-wide agreement was not established.
